USS Pegasus

USS Pegasus (AK-48 / IX-222)

Pegasus began life as the Danish freighter Rita Maersk and was acquired by the U.S. Navy in 1941. During the war she served as a cargo ship and later as a miscellaneous unclassified dry-cargo station ship.

By 1945-1946 Pegasus was operating in the Western Pacific, including Leyte Gulf in the Philippines. The diary follows Poppie's homeward trip as the ship returned to San Francisco.

Angelo Pignataro SC 3/c*

Account of my travels back home from the Philippines.

Written in diary form to Mom & Dad.

Notes & Details

Rating note

SC 3/C in a Navy context most likely means Ship's Cook, Third Class. SC = Ship's Cook; 3/C or 3c = Third Class. Full style: Ship's Cook Third Class. Roughly equivalent in rank level to Petty Officer Third Class, depending on the era. Modern closest successor: Culinary Specialist Third Class, CS3.

Jan. 4, 1946

We received orders today to return to the states & to report at San Francisco. It came as a surprise because we didn't get into dry dock yet. I can't explain how happy we all were. We needed fuel & water so we went to Leyte to get them. On the way the engine craped out & we were all pretty sore. In a few minutes the scuttlebutt was going around that it would take 12 days to fix it. There goes our chance of going home. Because as soon as the base command hears of it they'll tell us to stay here. But our captain didn't radio in & they went to work to fix it. Its now 10 P.M. & I guess I'll turn in.

Notes & Details

Leyte and liberation

Leyte is an island in the eastern Philippines. In this WWII context, liberation means the Allied campaign to free the Philippines from Japanese occupation; U.S. forces landed on Leyte on Oct. 20, 1944. Jan. 5, 1946

Awoke this morning & the first thing I heard was that the engines were fixed. That sounded pretty good. We got under way for Leyte again. We arrived at night & got filled up in a few hours. We'll have to leave tomorrow cause of some details. There weren't any movies tonight & the night was lonely & solemn. But I'll take it now that we're going stateside, I need to turn in.

Jan. 6, 1946

Today we get started for home at 1300. I went to work as usual. At 1330 we finally got started. Everyone was up getting their last look at the Philippines. (I hope) We're going pretty slow because of our bad engine. But as long as we're started we don't care. We got out of the harbor & into the open sea. In a short while we couldn't see any land. This was noon. Most of the men were talking of getting sea sick, & they did. They were heaving for hours. I can say truthfully I felt

Jan. 8, 1946

Sea still rough when I awoke. A couple of guys fell out of their sacks. Went to work & here I am. Voyage is pretty slow & long. We can't make good speed because it might hurt the engines. Jan. 9, 1946

Water calm now & the ship isn't rolling much. Today we're having target practice to use the ammunition up. I fired the 20 caliber rifle, 20 millimeter, 30 caliber machine guns, 3 inch gun & the 4 inch gun. The 3 & 4 make quite a bit of noise. The 4 inch has some flash too. Everyone got a chance at each gun. We had some ammo left over so they threw it over the side. The gunnery officer said we used more than $21,000 worth of ammo & threw away about $6,000 worth. We had orders not to bring any ammo back so we used it up.

Jan. 10, 1946

Well here it is Thursday & time to write today's account. Woke up this morning & the sea was pretty rough again. Its rolling like a pea would feel in a tub. If our ship was full of supplies I wonder how much better. Today we had a few drills. They were fire drill, abandon ship drill, & collision drill. Everyone has to wear a life jacket now because its so rough. They were issued to us the first day at sea. After work I went up for some more sun. Jan. 11, 1946

Woke up this morning at 6:30 & the sea was pretty calm. I hope it stays that way. Still making very slow progress. Rumors going around that we have to stop at Guam. I hope it doesn't delay our trip. I'm very anxious to get to the states. Last night's movie was "What a Woman" with B. O'Hara & Ross Russell. Enjoyed it a lot. So long till Sat nite.

Jan. 12, 1946

Well here it is Sat night & still out at sea. The sea been the roughest today than it ever was. The Captain told us we're going to Guam for sure now. It will delay us about 4 days. At least we might get liberty there. We need food supplies. We ran out of fruits 3 days ago & vegetables 2 days ago. Food is very low. It might be rationed. The men are flooding No. 1 & 5 holds to balance the ship. It's been rolling very bad. Lately, the water comes out of the water on every roll. It's been ruining the engine. We might also get a tug at Guam cause we're going so slow.

Jan. 13, 1946 - At Sea

Well, we've been at sea one week now & it seems like one year. Water still very rough but ship isn't rolling so much now that No. 1 & 5 holds are full of water. Its safer now. We'll arrive in Guam on Wed. We will get liberty there, water & food still rationed it's getting tougher now. Having movie tonight because we're going to Guam & we can get more of them. Good night.

Jan. 14, 1946 - At Sea

Well another day has passed & I'm getting closer to home. Thats all we talk about now. Today was payday because we're getting liberty in Guam & we need money. I didn't draw all my money. I'm saving it for when I come home. Then I can have a good time. We started an anchor pool today. We get 60 men to put up a dollar each. Then we got 60 sealed envelopes with numbers in them from one to 60. Every man puts his name on one of them. When we hit Guam & the anchor drops its recorded to the minute on the bridge. So if its 4:35 the man with the number 35 wins $60.00. We're starting one for Frisco soon for $5.00 per man. Thats a $300.00 prize. We will arrive in Guam tomorrow I think. Then we'll find out who has won.

I finished a good book last night. The name was "Valiant Libertine" by John Bryson. It took me quite a while to finish it because I only can read it at night. It's the longest book I ever read. It had 627 pages. I enjoy reading now. At one time I disliked it. Will tell tomorrow nite.

Jan. 15, 1946 - At Port, Guam

We arrived at Guam today after being at sea for 9 days. It was good to see land again. Guam is some place. It's the biggest naval base in the Pacific. There are over 100 ships in the harbor. We were anchored near the aircraft carriers "Hornet" & "Saratoga." Besides a few CVE's. We'll be here till Sunday then we'll begin our trip home again. We took the water out of No. 1 & 5 holds & were putting 150 aircraft engines in them. We're also getting supplies, water isn't rationed in port & now we have fresh water showers. Till tomorrow night!

Jan. 16, 1946 - At Port, Guam

I mailed your letter this morning & I know you'll be happy when you hear I'm coming home. I had a hard day today. Everyone had to work to clean out the 5 holds so we can put cargo in. I put in 8 hours in the galley & then 5 hours in the holds. When we were fueling up today I met a buddy from boot camp on the oiler tanker. His name is Bill Horner. He's stationed in Guam. He lives in Jersey. We moved to a pier today at 8:20 P.M. I met a fellow who was on here in Leyte. He was transferred. He's stationed here also. He has a jeep & I was driving it all night. It's now 11:30 so I was driving for about 3 hours. Those jeeps sure can go. Still loading tomorrow again. I hope we shove off Sunday. Good night. x

Jan. 17 - At Port, Guam

Well its now Thurs. night & I just came back from the movies. I had a full day today also. I worked from 8 to 12 in the holds & then went on liberty & then I worked in the galley from 3 to 7:30 P.M. It sure knocks a guy out. It sure is swell ashore on Guam. Swimming, cokes & beer, all you want. I swam all afternoon. Just like you see in the movies. A nice white beach - only no girls! Thurs. Feb. 7 - At Sea

I've became the tailor man on here. I've made $11.50 already sewing pants, rates & buttons. I can use the money cause I lost another $50.00 in craps.

Fri. Feb. 8 - At Sea

The ship going to the states with us broke down & we had to wait 6 hours for them. While we were waiting, we caught 2 sharks. We seen 100s whales. Tomorrow personnel inspection. White uniforms & all. Today we failed inspection in the galley. We got 12 hours extra duty cause of that. And we spent 6 hours last night cleaning up too. They haven't any heart. For the past 2 days we've all been freezing. And guess what the temperature was, only 79 degrees? Imagine freezing in that warm weather. But after spending 7 months in the Phil. with a temperature of 100 to 120 anything lower than that & we freeze. The doc's been giving us some pills so we won't catch cold. I don't know how we'll take it when we're in the states & its about 40 degrees. I use a blanket now.

59-day route check

The route shown totals about 6,650 nautical miles. Poppie's 59-day count lines up with Jan. 4, 1946, the diary's first homeward-order entry, through the Mar. 4 arrival in San Francisco. From the Jan. 6 departure from Leyte to Mar. 4 is about 57 elapsed days. Spread across 59 days, the mapped distance averages about 113 nautical miles per day, which fits his notes about slow speed, engine trouble, Guam and Pearl Harbor delays, and port days.
